# Takes

A take keeps the object and the colours of a direction and changes which of the object's properties carries the page. The Seed Lab connector's `variations` tool draws takes for one chosen object and returns each with its id, its key and its title. The paragraph under a key below is that take's `{take_text}` in the sketch, build and finishing instructions.

## structure: Structure leads

The object's divisions become the page's and nothing else is borrowed. Draw its grid, its rules and its compartments exactly, at the object's own proportions, and keep type and colour as quiet as the project's own. Someone who knows the object should recognise it from the layout with the styles switched off.

## scale: Scale leads

Take the one part of the object that is smallest in real life and most characteristic of it, and make it the largest thing on the first screen, filling most of the width. Everything else on the page is small and exact beside it. The page is remembered for that one jump in size.

## order: Order leads

Take the object's way of sequencing and referring to things: its numbering, its headings, its cross-references, the order in which it is read. Every block of content gets its place in that sequence, and a visitor moves through the page the way a reader moves through the object. The look stays close to plain.

## type: Type leads

Take the object's typographic system: how many sizes it uses, what is capitalised, what is tabular, how tight the lines are, where it changes weight. Set the whole page in that system with the typefaces the project already loads, or the ones set for this direction. Lettering that imitates the object appears in the largest headings only; nothing below them is in costume.

## colour: Colour leads

Take how the object uses colour, not which colours a picture of it shows: what a colour means there, how much of the surface it covers, what is left bare. Use the colours set for this direction as large flat fields that divide the page the way the object is divided, and keep the type plain on top of them.

## part: One part, repeated

Build the page from a single part of the object and nothing else: choose the part that can hold the most kinds of content, define it once, and make every block of content an instance of it at a different size. The first screen is one instance, very large.

## sparse: Nearly empty

Take the object at its barest: one entry of it, alone on the first screen with most of the surface left empty, and the rest of the page as few further entries as the content allows. What the object contributes is its restraint and its exact placement.
